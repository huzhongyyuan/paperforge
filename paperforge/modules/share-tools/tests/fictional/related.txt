**Text-to-video generation.** Text-to-video generation aims to synthesize videos that match both the content and the dynamics described by a prompt. Early diffusion-based methods add temporal layers to pretrained image models [CITE: inflated UNet video models], which keeps training tractable but limits long-range coherence. Video diffusion transformers [CITE: video DiT models] instead attend jointly over space and time, and have greatly improved complex motion. These models provide strong motion priors, but a text prompt alone is too coarse to specify an exact motion, which motivates transfer from a reference video.

**Video motion transfer.** Video motion transfer takes the motion from a reference video and applies it to new content. Training-free methods [CITE: attention-guidance motion transfer] extract motion representations from attention maps or features and steer the sampling process. They need no per-video optimization, but cannot express motion beyond the pretrained prior. Tuning-based methods [CITE: per-video LoRA or weight tuning] fit extra weights to each reference video, which improves fidelity at the cost of a long optimization and possible appearance leakage. Recent work reduces this cost by decoupling spatial and temporal parameters [CITE: spatial-temporal decoupled tuning].

**Frequency-aware generation.** Frequency decomposition has been used to separate structure from detail in image restoration and in diffusion sampling [CITE: frequency-aware diffusion]. These methods mainly use frequency as a fixed filter or a loss term on the output. Unlike these methods, our TideMotion uses frequency to decide which experts adapt to a reference video, so that adaptation itself is separated by band.
